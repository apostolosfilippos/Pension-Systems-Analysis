# Global Pension Systems - Data Insights & Sustainability

## 📊 Project Overview

I developed a Power BI dashboard to evaluate the fiscal efficiency of global pension structures. The analysis focuses on the trade-off between **Public Spending (% of GDP)** and **Net Income Replacement Rates**, providing a clear view of how different funding models impact state budgets and retiree adequacy.

## 🔍 Key Insights

**System Efficiency:** Funded models (private/individual accounts) achieve stable retirement income with a low public cost of ~4.2% GDP, whereas PAYG/NDC models often exceed 10–12% GDP to deliver similar adequacy levels. This efficiency gap reveals why countries facing aging populations and rising liabilities are transitioning toward Defined Contribution (DC) and Mixed models.

**Risk Mapping:** 39 countries analyzed and categorized into four performance quadrants. Greece and Italy emerge as high-expenditure outliers (>12% GDP public spending with below-average replacement rates), while the Netherlands and Denmark exemplify the "Mixed model" efficiency sweet spot—moderate public cost (~5–6% GDP) with strong income replacement (70%+).

**Demographic Pressure:** Countries with high old-age dependency ratios (Japan, Italy, Germany) show elevated public pension spending regardless of system type. Funded systems provide a structural advantage: they decouple benefit adequacy from government budgets, enabling solvency even in aging societies.

## 💡 Why This Matters

The 2008 financial crisis and 2020 pandemic exposed the fiscal fragility of pure PAYG systems. This analysis quantifies the cost of inaction: governments that delay structural reform face accelerating liabilities. Mixed and funded models demonstrate that pension adequacy and fiscal sustainability are not mutually exclusive—structural design matters more than spending alone.

## 🛠 Skills & Tools

**Dashboard Design:** Developed interactive quadrant charts to identify correlations between fiscal spending and benefit adequacy, enabling policymakers to benchmark their system's efficiency against peers.

**Data Synthesis:** Merged complex OECD financial metrics (public expenditure, replacement rates, coverage ratios) into a single, executive-ready visualization for strategic decision-making.

**Business Logic:** Applied demographic context (old-age dependency ratios, life expectancy) to explain why structural reform is urgent for aging economies, not optional.
