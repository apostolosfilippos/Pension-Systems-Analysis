Pension Systems Analysis: Sustainability & Adequacy
📊 Project Overview

This Power BI dashboard analyzes the correlation between Public Pension Spending and the Net Replacement Rate across 39 countries. The objective is to visualize how different pension architectures—PAYG, Mixed, and Funded—impact national budgets and retiree income levels.
🔍 Key Insights

  Fiscal Pressure & Efficiency: Funded systems prove significantly more cost-effective for the state, with public spending averaging ~4% of GDP. In contrast, traditional PAYG (Pay-As-You-Go) systems often exceed 13-15% of GDP, creating substantial fiscal strain.

   The Sustainability Gap: High replacement rates (e.g., Greece, Netherlands) can be achieved through various models, but the long-term fiscal burden varies dramatically. Systems relying solely on state funding face a "sustainability trap" in aging economies.

   Strategic Transition: My analysis indicates that transitioning from Defined Benefit (DB) to Defined Contribution (DC) models is a key lever for fiscal health. Such reforms can potentially reduce public pension expenditures by up to 17% while improving the system's resilience against demographic shifts.

   The "100-Year Life" Factor: As life expectancy increases, the data supports a shift toward hybrid models that encourage longer labor participation and private/occupational savings to maintain adequacy without overloading national budgets.

🛠 System Definitions

   PAYG (Pay-As-You-Go): A distributive model where current workers' contributions fund today’s retirees.

   Funded: Individual accounts where personal contributions are invested in financial markets to fund future pensions.

   Mixed: A hybrid architecture combining a basic state pension with mandatory or voluntary private savings.

📂 Data & Methodology

   Source: OECD (2024) Pensions at a Glance.

   Metrics: Public Pension Spending (% of GDP) vs. Net Replacement Rate (%) for average earners.

   Tools: Power BI, Excel, Data Modeling.
